## Reading the currents

The Hudson at Pier 66 is a tidal river, not a lake. Twice a day it floods (north, upriver) and twice it ebbs (south, downriver), and around slack water it briefly does neither. The stronger the current, the harder it is to hold position on the pitch — and the more serious a swim becomes, because a paddler out of their boat drifts with the river while everyone else is trying to reach them.

This is why our summer sessions carry level restrictions. If you are new, check with the session host before signing up, and respect any session marked Advanced/Expert Only. See the [schedule](/schedule/) for what each session type involves.
